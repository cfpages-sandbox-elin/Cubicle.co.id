# Topical Authority — cubicle.co.id

## Role and boundary

`cubicle.co.id` is an Indonesian umbrella reference and commercial-discovery property for three systems that share the English word *cubicle* but not the same engineering discipline:

1. office workstation cubicles and interior workstation partitions;
2. toilet cubicle partitions; and
3. enclosed low-voltage or medium-voltage electrical switchgear commonly called *cubicle listrik*.

This scope is evidenced by the homepage title, description, three service cards, and links to `office.cubicle.co.id`, `toilet.cubicle.co.id`, and `electric.cubicle.co.id`. The root should help owners, facility teams, designers, procurement teams, contractors, and technical reviewers identify which system they need, understand decisions and risks, prepare a defensible brief, and reach the right specialist route.

The site is not permitted to blur the three meanings. Architectural partition guidance cannot be reused as electrical-enclosure guidance. Office ergonomics does not establish toilet accessibility. Toilet moisture resistance does not establish fire performance. Electrical design, protection settings, energization, arc-flash analysis, and live work remain qualified-professional work.

Geography is Indonesia-wide. City/province swap pages are excluded. A local page is justified only by a real project, materially different authority requirement, climate/exposure condition, or measured supply condition.

Commercial route ownership:

- `/` owns umbrella selection, provider positioning, and routing among the three systems.
- `office.cubicle.co.id` owns office-cubicle quotation and service conversion.
- `toilet.cubicle.co.id` owns toilet-cubicle quotation and product conversion.
- `electric.cubicle.co.id` owns electrical-cubicle quotation and specialist conversion.
- Future educational article routes on `cubicle.co.id` own neutral reference intent and may link to a commercial route only when the reader has reached a relevant buying or service decision.

Related owned domains such as `cubicle.id`, `kubikel.co.id`, `kubikel.id`, `toiletcubicle.co.id`, and `toiletphenolic.co.id` are independent editorial properties. Cross-domain topic overlap is allowed. Cannibalization controls in these files apply only inside `cubicle.co.id`.

## Existing coverage and risks

The homepage provides useful commercial orientation but is too thin to answer selection, specification, safety, installation, or lifecycle questions. Its largest risk is semantic collapse: the page presents architectural panel materials immediately after three radically different systems, which can imply that glass, phenolic, wood, and PVC are materials for electrical cubicles. They are not the general material taxonomy for switchgear assemblies.

Other observed risks:

- “#1”, “100% guarantee”, a 4.9 rating, durability, and “experienced team” are unsupported in the repository; these require auditable evidence or toned-down language.
- `http://` links are used for the three specialist subdomains; use HTTPS after verifying each destination.
- WordPress search, feeds, REST, author, oEmbed, XML-RPC, and blank schema URLs remain in a static site and may create dead or misleading endpoint signals.
- Structured data labels the homepage as both `Article` and organization/person content, uses blank/relative URLs, and declares `en-US` while the page is Indonesian.
- The committed Rank Math XML is a backup artifact, not a deployable sitemap.
- Most image assets are unrelated starter-template media and should not be mistaken for original project evidence.
- There are no article templates, breadcrumbs, author/reviewer controls, revision dates, or internal-link system.
- Accessibility, fire, electrical, acoustic, structural, moisture, and chemical claims currently have no evidence gate.

Notable same-domain overlap groups to control are: “jenis cubicle” across three meanings; “material cubicle” between toilet and office partitions; “ukuran cubicle” between toilet stalls, workstations, and electrical enclosures; “panel cubicle” between architectural panels and switchgear panels; “instalasi cubicle” across building trades and electrical commissioning; “maintenance cubicle” across cleaning, reconfiguration, and electrical preventive maintenance.

## Evidence and editorial standards

### Universal evidence rules

- Date every regulation/standard status check. Verify again at drafting and material revision; do not assume this 2026-07-23 snapshot remains current.
- Prefer Indonesian government/JDIH, BSN, IEC/ISO, manufacturer test data, approved project documents, field measurements, and named competent reviewers.
- Never publish an exact standard clause from memory. Confirm the licensed text, edition, amendments, adoption, project specification, and authority interpretation.
- Separate mandatory law/regulation, adopted project standard, owner preference, manufacturer instruction, and editorial recommendation.
- Product ratings require a report/certificate tied to the exact tested product or assembly, thickness, support, fixing, joints, hardware, and test method. A component claim does not automatically transfer to an installed system.
- Prices, lead times, warranties, geographic service, ratings, testimonials, and case results require dated business evidence.
- “Waterproof”, “fireproof”, “soundproof”, “antibacterial”, “non-toxic”, “maintenance-free”, “arc-proof”, “premium”, “SNI”, or “certified” are prohibited without exact scope and proof.

### Qualified gates

| Gate | Publication requirement |
|---|---|
| Accessibility and dimensions | Check PP 16/2021, Permen PUPR 14/PRT/M/2017, applicable local rules, building function, project brief, and current adopted standards. Use dimensioned drawings and accessibility review. Never declare compliance from one generic stall dimension. |
| Fire and egress | Check PP 16/2021, Permen PU 26/PRT/M/2008, local fire rules, building classification, egress, sprinklers/detectors, and product/assembly test evidence. A material marketing label never establishes building-system compliance. |
| Structure and stability | Trace loads through panel, hardware, anchors, substrate, floor/wall/ceiling, and building structure. Require a structural or responsible design review for suspended systems, heavy loads, seismic restraints, questionable substrates, or safety-critical stability. |
| Electrical safety | Apply Permenaker 12/2015 as amended by 33/2015, PUIL/SNI 0225 as applicable, the correct IEC/SNI product family, owner/PLN rules, and competent authorized personnel. No DIY design, protection settings, testing, energization, live work, or internal access. |
| LV versus MV standards | IEC 61439-1 is general rules and must be paired with the relevant product part; it is not the MV metal-enclosed switchgear standard. IEC 62271-200 covers AC metal-enclosed equipment above 1 kV through 52 kV. Confirm adopted editions and amendments per project. |
| Arc and internal-fault claims | Require system study, installation configuration, accessibility class/test evidence where applicable, pressure/gas path, room interface, and competent review. PPE or an “arc-resistant” label is not a substitute for risk elimination and engineered controls. |
| Chemicals and cleaning | Obtain the current SDS and manufacturer compatibility/dilution instructions; apply workplace chemical-control and hazard-communication duties. Never recommend mixing chemicals. State ventilation, PPE, spill, storage, and stop conditions based on the actual product. |
| Ergonomics | Use current ISO 9241-5:2024 or the project’s adopted basis plus user/task measurements and a mock-up. Do not recycle the withdrawn 1998 edition as current or promise one-size-fits-all dimensions. |
| Acoustics and privacy | Distinguish absorption, sound isolation, masking, background noise, and visual/speech privacy. Use declared lab data or measured project data; do not infer privacy from panel height or soft finish alone. |
| Moisture and hygiene | Match exposure to core, edges, joints, hardware, sealants, cleaning regime, and ventilation. “Phenolic”, “PVC”, “glass”, or “stainless” alone does not prove durability or hygiene. |
| Case studies and reviews | Require permission, project identity or justified anonymization, dates, actual scope, before/after evidence, measurement method, limitations, and no fabricated experience. |

Electrical articles receive named qualified review before publication. Accessibility/fire/structural articles receive the applicable discipline review. Chemical instructions receive SDS-based review. Every high-stakes page carries a revision date, reviewer role, limitations, and emergency/stop conditions where relevant.
